# Audio concept: Nova as an instrument

Status: work in progress. Three instruments are live and ready for tuning: Strata, which replaced the three-voice synth, Fathom and Shoal; the [README](../README.md#audio) summarizes what it does. This document keeps the reasoning, the open questions and the offline [sketches](#sketches).

## Aim

Nova produces drone and ambient sound with its own character: one instrument that can be played with the sliders, not a set of per-module sound tracks. The reference is Biosphere, from the sustained, cold spaces of _Substrata_ to the pulsing patterns of _Microgravity_. Sliders and content together move the sound between cold and warm, cosmic and sci-fi, ambient and rhythmic.

Sound and light reference each other without being locked together: they share the controls and the tide clock, and the rendered image shapes the sound through slow, smoothed features, never by triggering notes voxel by voxel. The coupling stays subtle: a listener should sense that sound and light belong together, not hear the image being played.

Nova is not a general music engine or a DAW. It follows one specific approach to sound design, which opens up a large sonic space within clear boundaries. Instruments, voices and parameters are added where they widen that space, not for flexibility's sake, and the web app keeps its simple controls.

## Target system

Sound is designed for a high-end system with full bass extension, for example two Genelec 8030 with a 7050 subwoofer (down to about 25 Hz, bass-managed below about 85 Hz). Installations vary from living rooms to offices and exhibitions; Volume adapts the level, the design does not depend on the room.

What this implies:

- **Real sub content.** Drone roots and pulses may sit at 30–60 Hz.
- **Mono below about 120 Hz.** The subwoofer sums its channels; stereo decorrelation applies only above, otherwise the bass partly cancels.
- **Headroom.** Sub content dominates the peak budget: an infrasonic high-pass at 20 Hz, fixed gain staging, loudness-normalized layers and a clean look-ahead limiter instead of saturation of the whole mix.
- **Room modes.** Avoid a single static low sine; several close partials with slow beating and drift spread the bass across the room.
- **Transparency.** Monitors like these reveal artefacts: band-limited or additive oscillators, per-sample parameter smoothing, no aliasing from nonlinearities, a dense modulated reverb without metallic ringing.
- **Output hardware.** On the Raspberry Pi, a USB DAC or audio HAT with balanced outputs; the analog jack is not good enough.

## Layers

| Layer     | Method                                                                                                                                                    | Role                                    |
| --------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| **Bed**   | Additive partial bank in just intonation: each tone a set of partials with slowly beating pairs; the root is a synth bass with its own spectrum and level | The continuous drone                    |
| **Air**   | Pink noise through narrow resonators tuned to the bed's tones                                                                                             | Wind and breath, strongest when cold    |
| **Pulse** | Tuned sub thumps, noise ticks and filtered chord stabs on a step grid                                                                                     | Rhythm, from heartbeat to pattern       |
| **Space** | Long modulated FDN reverb plus a tempo-synced dub delay with a filter and saturation in the loop                                                          | Depth, distance and the signature sound |

These are Strata's layers. Fathom uses the same bed, air, kick, ticks and space, but plays the bed's chord as stabs instead of the pulse's own (see [Fathom](#fathom)).

Planned later: a **grain** layer (granular playback of buffers the instrument records from itself, later also samples), and possibly samples and spoken fragments. Samples are deliberately left out for now.

## Rhythm

Status: step 7 of the [plan](#plan). The conductor plays Strata's and Fathom's scores (`strata::SCORE`, `fathom::SCORE`), throws included; Fathom's patterns run from cold to warm, its echoes change with the sections, and it has fills, breaks and a bass line.

Rhythm must not feel random: its patterns come from dub techno and ambient music, and its variation from structure, not from chance.

The conductor plays the rhythm from a **score**, plain data in the instrument's preset: a few patterns each for the kick (Strata's thumps), the ticks, the stabs and the chord gate, later a bass line. At every bar start it reads the controls after the tides and decides which steps play; on every step it hands the instrument its hits. Instruments only give them sound, and answer the light with dynamics and accents, never with where steps fall. So the controls reach the rhythm in one place, and instruments that share the conductor play in the same time.

- A **pattern** gives every 16th a weight from 1 to 9, how essential the step is, written as a string: `"9... 5... 7... 5..."` is a kick that plays once per bar, then twice, then four to the floor; `.` rests and spaces only separate the beats. The weight also sets how hard a step plays. A pattern may span several bars, or run 7 or 12 steps against the bar.
- **Density** is how much of a pattern plays: the steps whose weight clears a threshold. Void sets it, through the rhythm; the kick, the ticks, the stabs and the gate each come in at their own point of it. A pattern grows by adding steps, never by moving them, and changes only at bar starts, with a little hysteresis.
- Each has its patterns in order, from straight and even (cold) to syncopated (warm), and Heat picks where between them it plays. Between neighbours the weights blend, so one pattern turns into the next step by step rather than as a crossfade of two rhythms. Heat, the harmony's sections and, later, a change of instrument all move through the patterns by this blend.
- A **phrase** is 4 bars. Throws mark its end, later fills and breaks, and the harmony's sections end on a phrase start.

Variation comes from structure:

- The rhythm follows the harmony: while the bass is away, every pattern moves to a warmer neighbour, and it returns home with the bass.
- The dub delay's time changes with the sections, from a dotted eighth to a quarter or a dotted quarter, and with it the rhythm of the echoes. The conductor decides which stabs are thrown; the score sets how often.
- The Heat tide moves through the patterns now and then; where the rhythm builds up or dissolves, the Void tide tips steps near the threshold, while on the plateau between it barely touches them. An audio tide, Looseness (43 s), varies the regularity rather than the speed: about half the time the rhythm is strict, otherwise ghost ticks appear on a few empty steps, a few ticks off the beat drop out and the swing grows slightly; kick and stabs stay exact. A dissolving rhythm loosens further.
- Fills: the last bar of every few plays every pattern one warmer. Breaks: the last phrase of every few lets kick, ticks and bass rest while every stab is thrown, so the echoes spiral; they drop back in with a big throw.
- Later: a pattern that shifts by a step every few phrases and comes back around, as in Reich's _Clapping Music_.

The patterns draw on this vocabulary:

|       | Straight (cold)                           | Syncopated (warm)                                                                            |
| ----- | ----------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Kick  | heartbeat, half time, four to the floor   | one drop (beat 3 only), a ghost kick before the bar                                          |
| Ticks | even 8ths or 16ths, accents on the beat   | off-beat hats, shaker 16ths accented on the off-beats, 3-3-2                                 |
| Stabs | one per bar, evenly spread                | skank (the off-beats), push (the 16th before a beat), 3-3-2, call and response over two bars |
| Bass  | the sustained bass, a pulse with the kick | the off-beat sub between kicks, a short dub riff (root, fifth, octave)                       |

How the controls and the sections move through the patterns is roughed in first and tuned by ear later.

## Fathom

The second instrument contrasts with Strata while it shares its world: Strata is a drone with a pulse under it, Fathom plays the drone's own chord as dub stabs, after Basic Channel, Rhythm & Sound and Deepchord. The bed's chord, folded into 180 to 720 Hz, plays through a resonant low-pass that opens and closes with each hit, and only the stab goes into the dub delay, so the echoes and a dark hall carry the groove. A deep sub bass and a soft kick stay underneath.

- **Stab**: an envelope attacks from where it is, so a retrigger never dips, holds for a fraction of a 16th and fades (`dsp::Contour`). The low-pass (`dsp::resonant_lowpass`: 24 dB per octave with an explicit resonance bump, applied to each partial, so it filters without aliasing) follows it: cold, it closes as the stab fades, a glassy resonant ping; warm, three times faster, a round "bwow". The resonance (6 dB when cold) falls away above 1.8 kHz and the cutoff stops at 3 kHz, so the pings stay soft in the highs; each stab opens the filter up to a fifth less than the last and hits up to a tenth softer, so a groove of them does not tire the ear. Form sets the length of the stabs (`length` in its chord's settings): attack, hold and release 2.5 times as long at 0, long and legato chords, and less than half as long at 1, short and clipped ones. The bed keeps the chord's loudness at the filter's most open state, so stabs hit equally hard whatever Heat, the palette or Void do, and the closed filter between them is naturally quieter.
- **Patterns** (`fathom::SCORE`), from the rhythm at bar starts with a hysteresis of 0.02, all on off-beat eighths, so swing never moves them; from about:

  | Rhythm | Stabs per bar                                      |
  | ------ | -------------------------------------------------- |
  | 0.15   | a swell on the second off-beat of every second bar |
  | 0.3    | the second off-beat (step 6 of 16)                 |
  | 0.42   | the second and fourth (steps 6 and 14): the skank  |
  | 0.58   | the second, third and fourth (steps 6, 10 and 14)  |
  | 0.72   | all four off-beats                                 |

  These are the coldest patterns. Warmer, the third stab pushes into the fourth beat (step 11), then the second bar answers the first with a stab right after the kick and one leading into the next bar; the kick gets a ghost before the bar and at the warmest a one drop, the ticks a shaker of 16ths. From a rhythm of 0.45 a bass line (`voices/sub.rs`) takes over the low end from the bed's sustained bass: on the off-beats between the kicks when cold, with octaves and fifths warmer, a dub riff on the one when warm. The last bar of every eighth is a fill, one pattern warmer, and the last phrase of every eighth a break, without kick, ticks and bass, every stab thrown. While the bass is away from home, every pattern moves one warmer (cooler from the warmest), the echoes go from dotted eighths to a quarter and, the next time, to a dotted quarter, and a slow ride opens the filter; back home it closes again. Sparser patterns hit a little harder, so the loudness holds as they thin out. The kick comes in last, from a rhythm of 0.4: once, then twice per bar, then four to the floor, never three, which would land on the stabs. Ticks join from 0.5. A bar plays only its strongest stab (`stabs` in the score; 4 lets every off-beat play, the skank): one stab a bar tires less, and a second head on the dub delay, two 16ths beyond the first as on a tape echo, lets its echoes follow irregularly. Three bass lines take turns, one per section of the harmony (`basses`): the off-beat line, a climbing one with the fifth, the octave and the seventh, and a spacious one of two notes a bar. The full rhythm, 0.8, plays all four off-beats over the kick four to the floor; dissolving, kick, ticks and bass leave first, and the stabs move into their echoes before they turn back into swells.

- **Drone**: below a rhythm of 0.15 (Void near 0, or near 1 once the rhythm has dissolved) the chord sustains through the low-pass, darker than Strata's drone and as loud, deeper in the hall than the stabs. The swells rise gently above it, their filter opening only brightening it. From a rhythm of 0.2 to 0.6 the drone gives way to the stabs, down to a residual 24 dB below, and the hall gets drier, so the stabs stand out from it. Towards Flow 0 the chord rests like the pulse and returns to the drone.
- **Throws**: the delay's feedback rises (up to 0.88) and its repeats get darker and more saturated for a moment; the thrown stab goes fully into the delay and lasts longer. The last stab of every fourth bar is thrown (every second while the bass is away), as is the first stab after the bass or the palette changes or after a sudden rise in visual activity, and now and then one when the rhythm loosens. `Throws::SHORT`, the default, suits short listening; `Throws::LONG` throws every 16 bars (8 while the bass is away), ignores onsets and rarely loosens, for hours.
- **Kick and echoes**: the kick dips the echoes and the hall, like a sidechain on a dub desk.

Questions for listening:

- Does the space carry the rhythm, or is the dry stab too present?
- Do the cold pings ring clearly without getting sharp?
- Do the throws feel intended, and how often for short and for long listening?
- Is the dark drone without rhythm a good counterpart to Strata's?

## Shoal

The third instrument is the most rhythmic and the most melodic: the chord on bells, as a gamelan would play it, after the interlocking parts of Balinese gamelan and the arpeggios of minimal music. Its rhythm and melody come from the same score as everything else; only the notes are new, degrees of the chord beside the weights.

- **Bells**: modal synthesis, four inharmonic modes per note (those of a free bar, 1, 2.76, 5.4 and 8.93), each with a twin detuned by 1.8 Hz when cold and 3.2 Hz when warm, so every note shimmers like a gamelan's paired instruments (ombak), the bed's beating twins struck. Eight notes ring at once; the quietest makes room for a new one, fading out over 3 ms. Modes above 2.5 kHz roll off by 24 dB per octave (`ceiling` in the bell's settings), so high degrees and bright palettes never ring piercingly: the octave around 4 kHz measures 6 to 7 dB lower than before. Form damps them, as gamelan players damp each note with the hand: they ring twice as long at 0 and a quarter as long at 1, struck harder there, so the upper modes come forward (`ring` and `mallet` in the bell's settings); the gong rings on.
- **Parts**: one pattern plays both interlocking parts, the even 16ths from the left and the odd ones from the right (as in the kotekan nyog cag). The patterns run from an arpeggio of 12 steps (cold), which phases against the bar, through interlocking parts circling the chord, to a syncopated figure over two bars (warm). Weights follow the levels of elaboration: quarters first, then eighths, then 16ths, so the rhythm doubles the density the way a gamelan moves between its levels (irama).
- **Gong**: the colotomic frame. The gong closes every phrase, its fifth marks the half; it plays the score's bass part.
- **Drone and beat**: the bed's chord sinks to about −7 dB under the bells; a soft kick and off-beat ticks join late, so Shoal can turn into dub without losing the bells.

Questions for listening:

- Do the bells shimmer without sounding out of tune?
- Does the arpeggio against the bar feel intended, and do the interlocking parts read as one stream?
- Is the gong a frame or a disturbance?

## The image

The image reaches the sound as a description of the light rather than of the display, so a projection or an LED wall could hand over the same, and later the sound could describe itself the same way for the light. Each feature is a contract about measuring, not about answering: every instrument decides what to do with it, rarely one to one, and may ignore it. A profile of the light over height is left out on purpose: ten bands in, ten bands out is a spectrum analyzer.

| Feature  | Measure                                                                               |
| -------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| level    | mean luminance                                                                        |
| balance  | where the light sits from left to right (along x, across all modules), −1 to 1        |
| width    | how far it spreads from left to right, 0 in one column, 1 spread evenly               |
| height   | where it sits from bottom to top, 0 to 1                                              |
| density  | share of lit voxels                                                                   |
| activity | relative change of the light per frame                                                |
| hues     | shares at 12 OKLCh hues 30° apart, weighted by chroma and luminance, 0 for gray light |

Neighbouring hues lie a fifth apart, as in the harmony: the bridge Scriabin's light organ built from keys to colors. For now only the image shapes the sound; feedback from the sound to the light is kept in mind. How strongly the sound answers at all is one weight (`IMAGE_WEIGHT` in the engine): it pulls the light towards a neutral one (`Features::NEUTRAL`: gray, half lit, centred, spread evenly, calm), so 0 ignores the image, onsets included, 1 answers it fully and more exaggerates it. The answers come one at a time, each a setting with 0 to ignore it:

1. Hues lean the chord tones whose colors show forward, the others back (`voicing` in the bed's settings, 3 dB), while the chord keeps its loudness. _(done)_
2. Height sets the brightness: Strata's chord tilts by up to 1.5 dB per octave (`height_tilt` in the bed's settings), the base of Fathom's filter ride moves by up to half an octave (`height` in its chord's settings). _(to listen to)_
3. Density as a complement for Fathom: as the lit share falls from half to nothing, the delay's feedback rises by up to 0.06 and the chance per bar of a throw by up to 0.25 (`sparse` in its chord's settings and in its score's throws). Much of it follows Void, which already sends more to the delay. _(to listen to)_
4. Balance sets where Fathom's echoes start, rather than panning the dry sound: once the light sits more than 0.1 off centre, they start on the side away from it, answering across the room, and stay there until it moves past the other side (`echo_side` in its chord's settings). _(to listen to)_
5. Level is not taken as such: it would fight the loudness normalization, and it follows Void and the share of lit voxels. Its other part is: the glow (`Features::glow`, the level over the share of lit voxels, how bright the lit voxels are), which Void leaves alone and which differs from module to module (0.37 for Oort Cloud to 0.65 for Betelgeuse with every control at 0.5). It brightens the hall: its damping moves by up to an octave either way of a glow of 0.5 (`glow` in the space's settings). _(to listen to)_
6. Width sets the stereo width above the bass: light gathered in one column narrows the side signal to 0.6, light spread out keeps it whole (`narrow` in the space's settings). With every control at 0.5 the modules measure 0.81 to 0.99, so it speaks only when the light gathers: at low Form in the modules of few bodies, at high Void, and later on LED walls. _(to listen to)_
7. Shoal answers like the others: visual activity strikes its bells up to a quarter harder or softer (`strike`), and the bells whose colors show ring forward, as the bed's chord tones do (`voicing`, 3 dB). _(to listen to)_
8. Density as a complement for Strata too: as the lit share falls from half to nothing, its air and hall grow by up to 3 dB (`sparse` in its preset). _(to listen to)_

## Constraints

The audio callback does not lock, allocate, log or touch files; controls are smoothed inside the engine; Volume is applied last with a click-free ramp; everything must run comfortably on a Raspberry Pi 4 next to the renderer. Void is shared with audio, and sound is loosely coupled to the image through features. The sketches compare Strata's score with a polymeter variant, ticks in loops of 12 steps and stabs in loops of 7 (`strata::Preset::POLYMETER`).

## Architecture

Audio is a crate of its own, `nova-audio` in `audio`, so it can grow without the host and be tested on its own; the [README](../README.md#audio) lists its layers and files. It is not a general music engine: it carries only what this approach to sound needs.

- **The bus connects.** The crate knows nothing of the host, nor of the display. It plays from the bus of `nova-core`, which it shares with the display (`nova-voxel`): the host puts the controls on it as set, the display adds the tide clock and the features of its light with every frame, and the sound applies the global tides itself, as the display does, and leaves its status there. Palettes and tides come from `nova-core` too. The sketches live in the crate; only they render the real content, through `nova-voxel` as a dev-dependency.
- **Layers by role.** `dsp` → `voices` → `instruments` → `engine` → `io`, each using only those before it. Voices know nothing of each other; an instrument plays voices together, and the engine plays the instrument.
- **A shared conductor.** Tempo, the grid of 16ths with its swing, bars, phrases, sections, the harmony's progression (including when a palette change may start), the audio tides and the playing of the instrument's score live in the engine rather than in an instrument, so several instruments can play in the same time, harmony and rhythm.
- **Typed signals.** Instead of a modulation matrix, a plain struct with one field per signal (`Signals`), written once per block: the conductor writes tempo, harmony and its changes, whether the bass is away, looseness and onsets; Strata's pulse writes the drone's dip, the chord gate and the bass accent; whoever needs a signal reads its field. Within an instrument, voices may still hand each other signals at audio rate (the drone follows the pulse sample by sample).
- **Presets.** An instrument's tuning constants are a plain data struct (`strata::Preset`, with settings for bed, pulse and space), so instruments in the web app can be presets of the same code; the sketches' polymeter variant already is one.
- **Fast in debug builds.** A package profile builds the crate with `opt-level` 2 in debug builds too. Instruments are an enum rather than trait objects, and the DSP stays plain code.

## Sketches

The sketches are an example of the audio crate, in `audio/sounddesign/sketches`; [Development](development.md#audio-output) has the commands. They render the instruments offline to `nova-audio-sketches` in the OS temporary directory, or to the directory in `NOVA_AUDIO_DIR`. Scripted scenarios set the controls as the sliders would, which the sound moves by the global tides as it does live, and hand over neutral gray light; the content modules are rendered by the real renderer through a bus, so they sound as the live instrument would.

Scenarios for Strata (`strata_…`): Heat sweeps, Void sweeps through the rhythm (in both rhythm variants) and through its dissolving, Flow sweeps, every palette in turn, four corners (cold and warm, ambient and rhythmic), 10 minutes of harmonic motion, the light from the bottom to the top, a Form sweep through the textures, sweeps of the lit share, the width and the glow of the light, and every content module with all controls at 0.5. Fathom has its own set (`fathom_…`): Heat with stabs, Void sweeps through the rhythm and its dissolving, Flow sweeps, every palette, the four corners, 10 minutes of harmonic motion, 3 minutes of its throws for long listening, the light from the bottom to the top, from all voxels lit to none, from the left to the right, a Form sweep, and every module at 0.5. Shoal's (`shoal_…`): Heat, Void and Form sweeps, a cold ambient and a warm rhythmic corner, 10 minutes of harmonic motion, and every module at 0.5. For each it writes a 32-bit float stereo WAV at 48 kHz and a spectrogram (PPM, 20 Hz to 20 kHz on a logarithmic axis), and `report.md` lists the integrated loudness (ITU-R BS.1770) and loudness range, the 95th percentile of the momentary loudness, the median and 95th percentile of the level between 1 and 4 kHz over 50 ms (where the ear is most sensitive, and how far hits stand out from the gaps), sample peak, crest factor, the share of energy per frequency band and the energy per octave band (the spectral balance), the side-to-mid ratio below 80 Hz (the mono check for the subwoofer), how much faster than real time the instrument rendered, the visual activity, and how many steps each pulse voice played.

With every control at 0.5, which plays the full rhythm, each instrument measures about −18.5 LUFS; the corners range from −21 to −16.7 LUFS, peaks stay below about −3 dBFS, and the side signal more than 40 dB below the mid signal under 80 Hz. Between 1 and 4 kHz, Fathom's stabs rise about 11 dB above its gaps, the other instruments' hits about 3 dB. On an Apple M2 they render about 50 times faster than real time in release builds, Fathom as fast as Strata. Debug builds optimize the audio crate too, which made the instrument 6 to 10 times faster there, close to its release speed. The reverb and the limiter are our own: an earlier FunDSP reverb, built from generic combinators, ran at 0.3 times real time in debug builds and made the stream drop out.

The measurements catch technical problems; they say nothing about whether it sounds good. Listening on the target system decides. Questions for listening:

- Does the drone have its own character, between cold and warm, without becoming a generic synth pad?
- Is the bass full but steady (no pumping from beating, no booming notes in the room)?
- Euclidean or polymeter: which rhythm feels intended rather than random?
- Do thumps, ticks and stabs sit in the drone, or on top of it? Is the dub delay too much or too little?
- Does Void at 0.5 sound like a neutral middle, and Void 1 far away but still present?
- Do the palettes give distinct tonal worlds, and do the crossfades (including the air resonators gliding) work?
- Are the modules distinguishable with all controls at 0.5, and is that coupling strong enough?

## Plan

1. Offline harness and first sketches: bed, air and space; pulse in Euclidean and polymeter variants. _(done)_
2. Go live: the renderer publishes features and effective controls to the audio thread, and the instrument replaces the three-voice synth. _(done)_
3. Listen and tune, step by step _(done for now)_:
   1. Tonal balance: fewer highs at their sources, the bed further back, a synth bass on the root.
   2. A warm bed that sounds less like an organ: breath, drifting brightness, slight detuning, deeper movement.
   3. Slow harmonic motion: root and voicing evolve over minutes.
   4. A narrower tempo range; tides vary the regularity of the rhythm rather than its speed.
   5. The drone follows the pulse: dips with the thumps, the synth bass accents them.
   6. Rhythmic chord modulation: the chord pulses with a gate pattern on the grid, while the bass is away from home.
   7. Visual activity on a logarithmic scale, so calm and busy modules both stay distinguishable.
4. Profile on the Raspberry Pi 4 with visuals active. _(done)_ The instrument renders at about 6 times real time (about 17% of one core), and the visuals take well under 1 ms per frame. Periods of 2048 frames stopped the underruns that periods of 512 caused. Listening on the target system in different rooms is still to come, through an audio HAT or USB DAC: the Pi's own output is 16-bit and noisy.
5. Architecture, without building a general music engine _(done)_: see [Architecture](#architecture). The instrument is now called Strata. The restructuring changed no sample: renderings before and after, live and driven by the content, are identical bit for bit, at the same speed in release builds.
   1. Move audio into its own crate, `nova-audio`: DSP, voices, the instrument and native output. It knows nothing of the server's state: the server adapts its settings, effective controls and image features to the crate's types. A package profile (`opt-level` 2 in dev builds) keeps debug builds fast enough for audio. The sketches stay in the server, since they need the content (since step 6.2 they live in the crate and render the content through `nova-voxel`).
   2. Inside the crate, structure by role: `dsp` (building blocks) → `voices` (bed, air, thump, tick, stab, reverb and so on) → `instruments` → `engine` (shared musical time, master, instrument selection) → `io`.
   3. A shared conductor: tempo, steps, bars, sections, harmony progression and tides move from the instrument into the engine, so several instruments can play in the same time.
   4. Typed signals instead of a modulation matrix (since called `Signals`, as `Bus` now names what the parts share): a plain struct (dip, gate, accent, away and so on), written per block and read by the instruments that need it.
   5. Presets: an instrument's constants become a plain data struct, so some instruments in the web app can be presets of the same code.
6. A second, contrasting instrument; then extract what both share.
   1. Fathom, the drone's chord as dub stabs (see [Fathom](#fathom)). _(done)_ The bed, the dub delay, the space and Strata's pulse gained settings and hooks with neutral defaults, so Strata's renderings stayed identical bit for bit. `instrument` in the settings file picks it until the web app has a selector.
   2. The image as a description of the light (see [The image](#the-image)), the sketches moved into the audio crate. _(done, to listen to)_ Its answers came one at a time.
   3. Extract what both share, once step 7.1 has taken the patterns and the throws out of the instruments: the space into a module of its own, and a beat (kick, ticks, the drone's dip and the bass accent) out of Strata's pulse, which Fathom now borrows with its stabs and chord gate off.
7. Rhythm: the conductor plays a score (see [Rhythm](#rhythm)), in steps to listen to; renderings need not stay identical.
   1. Scores and phrases _(done, to listen to)_: Strata's and Fathom's current patterns written as weights and played by the conductor, which also decides the throws, so Strata could throw its stabs too; the polymeter variant becomes patterns of 12 and 7 steps. The harmony's sections end on a phrase start.
   2. Fathom evolves _(done, to listen to)_: patterns from cold to warm, a warmer neighbour while the bass is away, the delay's time changing with the sections. Between neighbours, the steps of the next pattern come in during the first half of the way and those of the previous one leave during the second, so the blend never leaves a hole.
   3. Fills and breaks; the low end moving too: a bass line, and the kick taking part in the patterns' changes _(done, to listen to)_. The bass line's notes are intervals above the root, a second step string beside its weights.
   4. A rhythmic instrument, Shoal (see [Shoal](#shoal)): struck bars in pairs that beat like a gamelan's, with interlocking parts and arpeggios over the chord, through the dub delay. _(done, to listen to)_
8. Instruments follow the content _(first try, to listen to)_. Each content module plays an instrument (`instruments` in the settings file, defaults in `host/src/state.rs`); the host puts the selected module's on the bus. Selecting another module mixes over to its instrument in 5 s (`MIX_SECONDS`): the new one fades in as the old one fades out, both on the shared conductor, which turns to the new instrument's score on the first phrase start past halfway. Each instrument plays the parts of the current score it has voices for, so one may be thinner while it plays the other's: Shoal mixing in rings no bells until the score turns, Fathom mixing in from Strata plays Strata's stabs. The web app's debounce keeps quick cycling through the content from starting a mix. Every instrument is built up front, since the audio callback must not allocate; two play during a mix, which the Pi can afford (one takes about 17% of a core).
   - The features of the light do not pick the instrument: with every control at 0.5, the modules differ little in them (only activity and height show some character, and they drift), and they move with Void, Form and the palette, so an instrument chosen from them would change with the sliders and flip near a boundary. They keep shaping the sound within an instrument, and could later shape the mix, such as its pace.
   - No instrument selector in the web app: choosing the instrument belongs to the performer mode.
   - Loose ends: patterns that blend from one score to the other rather than switching at a phrase start, and the bass swapping on a phrase start; whether two beds playing the same chord sum well at equal power; an instrument mixing in again starts from where it stopped, its echoes and hall still decaying; a sketch of the mix; how long a mix should take; the links themselves, by ear.
9. The controls revisited. Rhythm matters most among the sonic qualities, and Form, which carried it, means structure in the light rather than gaps. The controls get one meaning each, shared by the instruments:

   | Control | Light           | Sound                                                                     |
   | ------- | --------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
   | Heat    | saturation      | color: glassy to round, straight to syncopated (unchanged)                |
   | Flow    | evolution rate  | time: tempo, evolution, sections (unchanged)                              |
   | Form    | layers to grain | texture: continuous to granular, in each instrument's own way             |
   | Void    | share left dark | gaps, in time (the rhythm) and in space (distance and a thinner spectrum) |

   Rhythm is void in time: without gaps the sound is one continuous mass, with some it falls into pulses, with mostly gaps into isolated, distant events. Heat stays the axis of the spectrum, so Form's texture is about continuity (envelopes, grains, damping), not a second brightness. The coupling to the light keeps to the controls and the features: no module hints. Each step is listened to on its own; levels are roughed in until step 9.3.

   1. Void plays the rhythm _(done, to listen to)_. A transfer curve (`dsp::Curve`: points, eased between each pair by a smoothstep, so it is flat at every point, never overshoots and has no kinks) maps Void to the rhythm: none at 0, full from 0.1 to 0.5, dissolving towards 1. The default of 0.5 sits on a flat shoulder, so the Void tide only touches the weakest steps. Everything that followed Form as "how rhythmic" follows the rhythm: the score's density and the amounts that come with it (Strata's ducking and chord gate, Fathom's drone giving way to its stabs, Shoal's drone sinking under the bells). Dissolving, the parts leave in the reverse order they came in (Strata keeps a distant heartbeat longest, Fathom returns to swells and drone, Shoal's elaboration halves down to the gong), while Void's distance grows as before; the rhythm also loses its grip (looseness rises) and stabs move into their echoes. Full is what Form 0.8 played before: four to the floor with eighths of ticks in Strata; the densest steps are left out. Form did nothing in the sound until step 9.2.
   2. Form shapes the texture _(done, to listen to)_, from layers to grain, mostly with parameters that exist: Shoal's bells from ringing to damped, as gamelan players damp each note with the hand (decay, damping of the upper modes, a harder mallet); Fathom's stabs from long, legato chords to short, clipped ones; Strata's drone from smooth partials to grains (partials breaking into short bursts) and its air from breath to dust (sparse impulses into the resonators).
   3. Balance the instruments. With every control at 0.5, Fathom measures 2 dB below Strata in integrated loudness (−21 against −19 LUFS, Shoal −21), but its stabs peak 4 to 6 dB above the others between 1 and 4 kHz and 16 dB above its own gaps (Strata 4 dB, Shoal 3 dB): dark gaps and bright, resonant hits on every off-beat make it dominant and tiring. The report gains the momentary loudness and the peaks of that band; Fathom's filter opens less and less high, rings less, and its stabs stand out less from the gaps and vary slightly; then all three meet at the same loudness and comparable peaks, checked on long renderings for fatigue. _(done, to listen to)_ Each preset has an output `level` in dB. With every control at 0.5 all three now measure −18.5 LUFS (momentary 95th percentile −16.4) and peak at −27 to −28 dB between 1 and 4 kHz; Fathom's gaps stay darker (−39 against −31 dB), as dub stabs are. In the corners the instruments stay within about 1 dB of each other, but warm rhythmic scenes measure 3 to 4 dB louder than cold ones in each: left for the final listening session, with the fatigue on the long renderings (`fathom_10_harmonic_motion`, `fathom_11_long_listening`).
   4. The image _(done, to listen to)_. A weight for the whole coupling (`IMAGE_WEIGHT` in the engine) pulls the smoothed light, onsets included, towards a neutral light: 0 ignores the image, 1 answers it fully. Then new answers, each a setting with 0 to ignore it: Shoal's bells strike harder with visual activity, and those whose colors show ring forward, like the bed's chord tones; sparse light fills Strata's space with air and hall, as it fills Fathom's with echoes; the glow of the light (its level over the share of lit voxels, how bright the lit voxels are, which Void leaves alone) brightens the hall; its width sets the stereo width above the mono bass, narrow light a focused image.
   5. The loose ends of the mix from step 8.
