# Audio concept: Nova as an instrument

Status: work in progress. The instrument is live (it replaced the three-voice synth) and ready for tuning; the [README](../README.md#audio) summarizes what it does. This document keeps the reasoning, the open questions and the offline [sketches](#sketches).

## Aim

Nova produces drone and ambient sound with its own character: one instrument that can be played with the sliders, not a set of per-module sound tracks. The reference is Biosphere, from the sustained, cold spaces of _Substrata_ to the pulsing patterns of _Microgravity_. Sliders and content together move the sound between cold and warm, cosmic and sci-fi, ambient and rhythmic.

Sound and light reference each other without being locked together: they share the controls and the tide clock, and the rendered image shapes the sound through slow, smoothed features, never by triggering notes voxel by voxel. The coupling stays subtle: a listener should sense that sound and light belong together, not hear the image being played.

Nova is not a general music engine or a DAW. It follows one specific approach to sound design, which opens up a large sonic space within clear boundaries. Instruments, voices and parameters are added where they widen that space, not for flexibility's sake, and the web app keeps its simple controls.

## Target system

Sound is designed for a high-end system with full bass extension, for example two Genelec 8030 with a 7050 subwoofer (down to about 25 Hz, bass-managed below about 85 Hz). Installations vary from living rooms to offices and exhibitions; Volume adapts the level, the design does not depend on the room.

What this implies:

- **Real sub content.** Drone roots and pulses may sit at 30–60 Hz.
- **Mono below about 120 Hz.** The subwoofer sums its channels; stereo decorrelation applies only above, otherwise the bass partly cancels.
- **Headroom.** Sub content dominates the peak budget: an infrasonic high-pass at 20 Hz, fixed gain staging, loudness-normalized layers and a clean look-ahead limiter instead of saturation of the whole mix.
- **Room modes.** Avoid a single static low sine; several close partials with slow beating and drift spread the bass across the room.
- **Transparency.** Monitors like these reveal artefacts: band-limited or additive oscillators, per-sample parameter smoothing, no aliasing from nonlinearities, a dense modulated reverb without metallic ringing.
- **Output hardware.** On the Raspberry Pi, a USB DAC or audio HAT with balanced outputs; the analog jack is not good enough.

## Layers

| Layer     | Method                                                                                                                                                    | Role                                    |
| --------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| **Bed**   | Additive partial bank in just intonation: each tone a set of partials with slowly beating pairs; the root is a synth bass with its own spectrum and level | The continuous drone                    |
| **Air**   | Pink noise through narrow resonators tuned to the bed's tones                                                                                             | Wind and breath, strongest when cold    |
| **Pulse** | Tuned sub thumps, noise ticks and filtered chord stabs on a step grid                                                                                     | Rhythm, from heartbeat to pattern       |
| **Space** | Long modulated FDN reverb plus a tempo-synced dub delay with a filter and saturation in the loop                                                          | Depth, distance and the signature sound |

Planned later: a **grain** layer (granular playback of buffers the instrument records from itself, later also samples), and possibly samples and spoken fragments. Samples are deliberately left out for now.

## Mapping

| **Palette**        | The tonal world. The chroma-weighted mean hue picks the root on the circle of fifths; every color adds a tone whose interval follows its hue distance to the mean (close hues give fifths and fourths, contrasting hues thirds and sixths), its lightness the octave, and its chroma the level. A palette change crossfades to the new tones. The harmony moves in sections: at every boundary the bass glides (8 s) between home and the palette's own pitch classes (fifth, fourth, third and so on, in that order of preference; the fourth and fifth if its tones are all octaves of the root), so the chord above stays consonant and changes its color; halfway through a section, one chord tone fades out and returns an octave higher or lower.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |

Warm is round: strong low harmonics with rolled-off highs, the lower tones in front, wider beating and a gentle wow, breath around the chord tones, a slowly drifting brightness per tone, slightly detuned upper partials and tones that fade back now and then, soft breathy air, swing, longer and slightly saturated thumps, darker stabs and more dub feedback. ## Rhythm

Rhythm must not feel random. The backbone is deterministic, and variation is slow and controlled:

1. **Euclidean patterns**: Form sets how many pulses are spread evenly over 16 steps.
2. **Polymeter**: loops of different lengths (16, 12 and 7 steps) phase against each other, for long but predictable cycles.
3. Later, if needed: **repetition with slow mutation** (a seeded pattern repeats for many bars and changes one step at a time).

Image features change dynamics and accents, not where the steps fall. Variants 1 and 2 are rendered side by side for comparison; the live instrument plays Euclidean patterns. An audio tide, Looseness (43 s, see `tides.rs`), varies the regularity rather than the speed: about half the time the rhythm is strict, otherwise ghost ticks appear on a few empty steps, a few ticks off the beat drop out and the swing grows slightly; thumps and stabs stay exact.

## Constraints

The audio callback does not lock, allocate, log or touch files; controls are smoothed inside the instrument; Volume is applied last with a click-free ramp; everything must run comfortably on a Raspberry Pi 4 next to the renderer. Void is shared with audio, and sound is loosely coupled to the image through features. The live instrument plays the Euclidean rhythm (`RHYTHM` in `audio/output.rs`); the sketches compare both.

## Sketches

`cargo test --release audio_sketches -- --ignored --nocapture` in `server` renders the instrument offline, driven by the real content modules, tides and image features, to `nova-audio-sketches` in the OS temporary directory, or to the directory in `NOVA_AUDIO_DIR`. `NOVA_SKETCH` limits the run to scenarios whose name contains its value.

Scenarios: Heat, Form (in both rhythm variants), Void and Flow sweeps, every palette in turn, four corners (cold and warm, ambient and rhythmic), 10 minutes of harmonic motion, and every content module with all controls at 0.5. For each it writes a 32-bit float stereo WAV at 48 kHz and a spectrogram (PPM, 20 Hz to 20 kHz on a logarithmic axis), and `report.md` lists the integrated loudness (ITU-R BS.1770) and loudness range, sample peak, crest factor, the share of energy per frequency band and the energy per octave band (the spectral balance), the side-to-mid ratio below 80 Hz (the mono check for the subwoofer), how much faster than real time the instrument rendered, the visual activity, and how many steps each pulse voice played.

The sketches measure about −21 LUFS for the drone and −19.5 LUFS with the pulse, peaks below −3 dBFS, and side signal more than 40 dB below the mid signal under 80 Hz. On an Apple M2 they render about 50 times faster than real time in release builds and 3 times in debug builds. The reverb and the limiter are our own: an earlier FunDSP reverb, built from generic combinators, ran at 0.3 times real time in debug builds and made the stream drop out.

The measurements catch technical problems; they say nothing about whether it sounds good. Listening on the target system decides. Questions for listening:

- Does the drone have its own character, between cold and warm, without becoming a generic synth pad?
- Is the bass full but steady (no pumping from beating, no booming notes in the room)?
- Euclidean or polymeter: which rhythm feels intended rather than random?
- Do thumps, ticks and stabs sit in the drone, or on top of it? Is the dub delay too much or too little?
- Does Void at 0.5 sound like a neutral middle, and Void 1 far away but still present?
- Do the palettes give distinct tonal worlds, and do the crossfades (including the air resonators gliding) work?
- Are the modules distinguishable with all controls at 0.5, and is that coupling strong enough?

## Plan

1. Offline harness and first sketches: bed, air and space; pulse in Euclidean and polymeter variants. _(done)_
2. Go live: the renderer publishes features and effective controls to the audio thread, and the instrument replaces the three-voice synth. _(done)_
3. Listen and tune, step by step _(done for now)_:
   1. Tonal balance: fewer highs at their sources, the bed further back, a synth bass on the root.
   2. A warm bed that sounds less like an organ: breath, drifting brightness, slight detuning, deeper movement.
   3. Slow harmonic motion: root and voicing evolve over minutes.
   4. A narrower tempo range; tides vary the regularity of the rhythm rather than its speed.
   5. The drone follows the pulse: dips with the thumps, the synth bass accents them.
   6. Rhythmic chord modulation: the chord pulses with a gate pattern on the grid, while the bass is away from home.
   7. Visual activity on a logarithmic scale, so calm and busy modules both stay distinguishable.
4. Profile on the Raspberry Pi 4 with visuals active. _(done)_ The instrument renders at about 6 times real time (about 17% of one core), and the visuals take well under 1 ms per frame. Periods of 2048 frames stopped the underruns that periods of 512 caused. Listening on the target system in different rooms is still to come, through an audio HAT or USB DAC: the Pi's own output is 16-bit and noisy.
5. Architecture, without building a general music engine _(next)_:
   1. Move audio into its own crate, `nova-audio`: DSP, voices, the instrument and native output. It knows nothing of the server's state: the server adapts its settings, effective controls and image features to the crate's types. A package profile (`opt-level` 2 in dev builds) keeps debug builds fast enough for audio. The sketches stay in the server, since they need the content.
   2. Inside the crate, structure by role: `dsp` (building blocks) → `voices` (bed, air, thump, tick, stab, reverb and so on) → `instruments` → `engine` (shared musical time, master, instrument selection) → `io`.
   3. A shared conductor: tempo, steps, bars, sections, harmony progression and tides move from the instrument into the engine, so several instruments can play in the same time.
   4. A typed signal bus instead of a modulation matrix: a plain struct (dip, gate, accent, away and so on), written per block and read by the instruments that need it.
   5. Presets: an instrument's constants become a plain data struct, so some instruments in the web app can be presets of the same code.
6. A second, contrasting instrument; then extract what both share.
7. An instrument selector in the web app, like the content strip, once there are two: every instrument interprets the same controls, instruments are registered like content modules, and switching crossfades.
8. Later:
   - Tuning: reverb, delay and stereo image; dub throws of the chord; the pulse into the fog (less transient thumps and ticks, deeper with Void); a bass accent that stays out of the chord's range; looseness for the drone's pumping; `COUPLING` as a parameter; a per-room low-pass setting in the settings file; the grain layer.
   - The image, always subtle: height sets the chord's register, brightness and width; the colours' shares weight the chord tones; local flashes as onsets; features that follow Flip Vertical; module hints.
   - Output: choose the highest-resolution sample format a device offers (F32, I32 or I24 before I16), with dither for 16-bit output; scale quality down (reverb channels, partials) only if a target needs it.
