# Audio concept: Nova as an instrument

Status: work in progress. Two instruments are live and ready for tuning: Strata, which replaced the three-voice synth, and Fathom; the [README](../README.md#audio) summarizes what it does. This document keeps the reasoning, the open questions and the offline [sketches](#sketches).

## Aim

Nova produces drone and ambient sound with its own character: one instrument that can be played with the sliders, not a set of per-module sound tracks. The reference is Biosphere, from the sustained, cold spaces of _Substrata_ to the pulsing patterns of _Microgravity_. Sliders and content together move the sound between cold and warm, cosmic and sci-fi, ambient and rhythmic.

Sound and light reference each other without being locked together: they share the controls and the tide clock, and the rendered image shapes the sound through slow, smoothed features, never by triggering notes voxel by voxel. The coupling stays subtle: a listener should sense that sound and light belong together, not hear the image being played.

Nova is not a general music engine or a DAW. It follows one specific approach to sound design, which opens up a large sonic space within clear boundaries. Instruments, voices and parameters are added where they widen that space, not for flexibility's sake, and the web app keeps its simple controls.

## Target system

Sound is designed for a high-end system with full bass extension, for example two Genelec 8030 with a 7050 subwoofer (down to about 25 Hz, bass-managed below about 85 Hz). Installations vary from living rooms to offices and exhibitions; Volume adapts the level, the design does not depend on the room.

What this implies:

- **Real sub content.** Drone roots and pulses may sit at 30–60 Hz.
- **Mono below about 120 Hz.** The subwoofer sums its channels; stereo decorrelation applies only above, otherwise the bass partly cancels.
- **Headroom.** Sub content dominates the peak budget: an infrasonic high-pass at 20 Hz, fixed gain staging, loudness-normalized layers and a clean look-ahead limiter instead of saturation of the whole mix.
- **Room modes.** Avoid a single static low sine; several close partials with slow beating and drift spread the bass across the room.
- **Transparency.** Monitors like these reveal artefacts: band-limited or additive oscillators, per-sample parameter smoothing, no aliasing from nonlinearities, a dense modulated reverb without metallic ringing.
- **Output hardware.** On the Raspberry Pi, a USB DAC or audio HAT with balanced outputs; the analog jack is not good enough.

## Layers

| Layer     | Method                                                                                                                                                    | Role                                    |
| --------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| **Bed**   | Additive partial bank in just intonation: each tone a set of partials with slowly beating pairs; the root is a synth bass with its own spectrum and level | The continuous drone                    |
| **Air**   | Pink noise through narrow resonators tuned to the bed's tones                                                                                             | Wind and breath, strongest when cold    |
| **Pulse** | Tuned sub thumps, noise ticks and filtered chord stabs on a step grid                                                                                     | Rhythm, from heartbeat to pattern       |
| **Space** | Long modulated FDN reverb plus a tempo-synced dub delay with a filter and saturation in the loop                                                          | Depth, distance and the signature sound |

These are Strata's layers. Fathom uses the same bed, air, kick, ticks and space, but plays the bed's chord as stabs instead of the pulse's own (see [Fathom](#fathom)).

Planned later: a **grain** layer (granular playback of buffers the instrument records from itself, later also samples), and possibly samples and spoken fragments. Samples are deliberately left out for now.

## Rhythm

Status: step 7 of the [plan](#plan). The conductor plays Strata's and Fathom's scores (`strata::SCORE`, `fathom::SCORE`), throws included; Fathom's patterns run from cold to warm, its echoes change with the sections, and it has fills, breaks and a bass line.

Rhythm must not feel random: its patterns come from dub techno and ambient music, and its variation from structure, not from chance.

The conductor plays the rhythm from a **score**, plain data in the instrument's preset: a few patterns each for the kick (Strata's thumps), the ticks, the stabs and the chord gate, later a bass line. At every bar start it reads the controls after the tides and decides which steps play; on every step it hands the instrument its hits. Instruments only give them sound, and answer the light with dynamics and accents, never with where steps fall. So the controls reach the rhythm in one place, and instruments that share the conductor play in the same time.

- A **pattern** gives every 16th a weight from 1 to 9, how essential the step is, written as a string: `"9... 5... 7... 5..."` is a kick that plays once per bar, then twice, then four to the floor; `.` rests and spaces only separate the beats. The weight also sets how hard a step plays. A pattern may span several bars, or run 7 or 12 steps against the bar.
- **Density** is how much of a pattern plays: the steps whose weight clears a threshold. Form sets it; the kick, the ticks, the stabs and the gate each come in at their own Form. A pattern grows by adding steps, never by moving them, and changes only at bar starts, with a little hysteresis.
- Each has its patterns in order, from straight and even (cold) to syncopated (warm), and Heat picks where between them it plays. Between neighbours the weights blend, so one pattern turns into the next step by step rather than as a crossfade of two rhythms. Heat, the harmony's sections and, later, a change of instrument all move through the patterns by this blend.
- A **phrase** is 4 bars. Throws mark its end, later fills and breaks, and the harmony's sections end on a phrase start.

Variation comes from structure:

- The rhythm follows the harmony: while the bass is away, every pattern moves to a warmer neighbour, and it returns home with the bass.
- The dub delay's time changes with the sections, from a dotted eighth to a quarter or a dotted quarter, and with it the rhythm of the echoes. The conductor decides which stabs are thrown; the score sets how often.
- The Heat and Form tides tip steps near the threshold now and then. An audio tide, Looseness (43 s), varies the regularity rather than the speed: about half the time the rhythm is strict, otherwise ghost ticks appear on a few empty steps, a few ticks off the beat drop out and the swing grows slightly; kick and stabs stay exact.
- Fills: the last bar of every few plays every pattern one warmer. Breaks: the last phrase of every few lets kick, ticks and bass rest while every stab is thrown, so the echoes spiral; they drop back in with a big throw.
- Later: a pattern that shifts by a step every few phrases and comes back around, as in Reich's _Clapping Music_.

The patterns draw on this vocabulary:

|       | Straight (cold)                           | Syncopated (warm)                                                                            |
| ----- | ----------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Kick  | heartbeat, half time, four to the floor   | one drop (beat 3 only), a ghost kick before the bar                                          |
| Ticks | even 8ths or 16ths, accents on the beat   | off-beat hats, shaker 16ths accented on the off-beats, 3-3-2                                 |
| Stabs | one per bar, evenly spread                | skank (the off-beats), push (the 16th before a beat), 3-3-2, call and response over two bars |
| Bass  | the sustained bass, a pulse with the kick | the off-beat sub between kicks, a short dub riff (root, fifth, octave)                       |

How the controls and the sections move through the patterns is roughed in first and tuned by ear later.

## Fathom

The second instrument contrasts with Strata while it shares its world: Strata is a drone with a pulse under it, Fathom plays the drone's own chord as dub stabs, after Basic Channel, Rhythm & Sound and Deepchord. The bed's chord, folded into 180 to 720 Hz, plays through a resonant low-pass that opens and closes with each hit, and only the stab goes into the dub delay, so the echoes and a dark hall carry the groove. A deep sub bass and a soft kick stay underneath.

- **Stab**: an envelope attacks from where it is, so a retrigger never dips, holds for a fraction of a 16th and fades (`dsp::Contour`). The low-pass (`dsp::resonant_lowpass`: 24 dB per octave with an explicit resonance bump, applied to each partial, so it filters without aliasing) follows it: cold, it closes as the stab fades, a glassy resonant ping; warm, three times faster, a round "bwow". The resonance falls away above 2.5 kHz and the cutoff stops at 4.5 kHz, so the pings stay soft in the highs. The bed keeps the chord's loudness at the filter's most open state, so stabs hit equally hard whatever Heat, the palette or Void do, and the closed filter between them is naturally quieter.
- **Patterns** (`fathom::SCORE`), from Form at bar starts with a hysteresis of 0.02, all on off-beat eighths, so swing never moves them; from about:

  | Form | Stabs per bar                                      |
  | ---- | -------------------------------------------------- |
  | 0.15 | a swell on the second off-beat of every second bar |
  | 0.3  | the second off-beat (step 6 of 16)                 |
  | 0.42 | the second and fourth (steps 6 and 14): the skank  |
  | 0.58 | the second, third and fourth (steps 6, 10 and 14)  |
  | 0.72 | all four off-beats                                 |

  These are the coldest patterns. Warmer, the third stab pushes into the fourth beat (step 11), then the second bar answers the first with a stab right after the kick and one leading into the next bar; the kick gets a ghost before the bar and at the warmest a one drop, the ticks a shaker of 16ths. From Form 0.45 a bass line (`voices/sub.rs`) takes over the low end from the bed's sustained bass: on the off-beats between the kicks when cold, with octaves and fifths warmer, a dub riff on the one when warm. The last bar of every eighth is a fill, one pattern warmer, and the last phrase of every eighth a break, without kick, ticks and bass, every stab thrown. While the bass is away from home, every pattern moves one warmer (cooler from the warmest), the echoes go from dotted eighths to a quarter and, the next time, to a dotted quarter, and a slow ride opens the filter; back home it closes again. Sparser patterns hit a little harder, so the loudness holds as they thin out. The kick comes in last, from Form 0.4: once, then twice per bar, then four to the floor, never three, which would land on the stabs. Ticks join from 0.5.

- **Drone**: below Form 0.15 the chord sustains through the low-pass, darker than Strata's drone and as loud, deeper in the hall than the stabs. The swells rise gently above it, their filter opening only brightening it. From Form 0.2 to 0.6 the drone gives way to the stabs, down to a residual 24 dB below, and the hall gets drier, so the stabs stand out from it. Towards Flow 0 the chord rests like the pulse and returns to the drone.
- **Throws**: the delay's feedback rises (up to 0.88) and its repeats get darker and more saturated for a moment; the thrown stab goes fully into the delay and lasts longer. The last stab of every fourth bar is thrown (every second while the bass is away), as is the first stab after the bass or the palette changes or after a sudden rise in visual activity, and now and then one when the rhythm loosens. `Throws::SHORT`, the default, suits short listening; `Throws::LONG` throws every 16 bars (8 while the bass is away), ignores onsets and rarely loosens, for hours.
- **Kick and echoes**: the kick dips the echoes and the hall, like a sidechain on a dub desk.

Questions for listening:

- Does the space carry the rhythm, or is the dry stab too present?
- Do the cold pings ring clearly without getting sharp?
- Do the throws feel intended, and how often for short and for long listening?
- Is the dark drone at low Form a good counterpart to Strata's?

## The image

The image reaches the sound as a description of the light rather than of the display, so a projection or an LED wall could hand over the same, and later the sound could describe itself the same way for the light. Each feature is a contract about measuring, not about answering: every instrument decides what to do with it, rarely one to one, and may ignore it. A profile of the light over height is left out on purpose: ten bands in, ten bands out is a spectrum analyzer.

| Feature  | Measure                                                                               |
| -------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| level    | mean luminance                                                                        |
| balance  | where the light sits from left to right (along x, across all modules), −1 to 1        |
| width    | how far it spreads from left to right, 0 in one column, 1 spread evenly               |
| height   | where it sits from bottom to top, 0 to 1                                              |
| density  | share of lit voxels                                                                   |
| activity | relative change of the light per frame                                                |
| hues     | shares at 12 OKLCh hues 30° apart, weighted by chroma and luminance, 0 for gray light |

Neighbouring hues lie a fifth apart, as in the harmony: the bridge Scriabin's light organ built from keys to colors. For now only the image shapes the sound; feedback from the sound to the light is kept in mind. The answers come one at a time, each a setting with 0 to ignore it:

1. Hues lean the chord tones whose colors show forward, the others back (`voicing` in the bed's settings, 3 dB), while the chord keeps its loudness. _(done)_
2. Height sets the brightness: Strata's chord tilts by up to 1.5 dB per octave (`height_tilt` in the bed's settings), the base of Fathom's filter ride moves by up to half an octave (`height` in its chord's settings). _(to listen to)_
3. Density as a complement for Fathom: as the lit share falls from half to nothing, the delay's feedback rises by up to 0.06 and the chance per bar of a throw by up to 0.25 (`sparse` in its chord's settings and in its score's throws). Much of it follows Void, which already sends more to the delay. _(to listen to)_
4. Balance sets where Fathom's echoes start, rather than panning the dry sound: once the light sits more than 0.1 off centre, they start on the side away from it, answering across the room, and stay there until it moves past the other side (`echo_side` in its chord's settings). _(to listen to)_
5. Level and width are ignored for now: level would fight the loudness normalization and Brightness, and width says little next to balance on a single module. _(done)_

## Constraints

The audio callback does not lock, allocate, log or touch files; controls are smoothed inside the engine; Volume is applied last with a click-free ramp; everything must run comfortably on a Raspberry Pi 4 next to the renderer. Void is shared with audio, and sound is loosely coupled to the image through features. The sketches compare Strata's score with a polymeter variant, ticks in loops of 12 steps and stabs in loops of 7 (`strata::Preset::POLYMETER`).

## Architecture

Audio is a crate of its own, `nova-audio` in `audio`, so it can grow without the host and be tested on its own; the [README](../README.md#audio) lists its layers and files. It is not a general music engine: it carries only what this approach to sound needs.

- **The bus connects.** The crate knows nothing of the host, nor of the display. It plays from the bus of `nova-core`, which it shares with the display (`nova-voxel`): the host puts the controls on it as set, the display adds the tide clock and the features of its light with every frame, and the sound applies the global tides itself, as the display does, and leaves its status there. Palettes and tides come from `nova-core` too. The sketches live in the crate; only they render the real content, through `nova-voxel` as a dev-dependency.
- **Layers by role.** `dsp` → `voices` → `instruments` → `engine` → `io`, each using only those before it. Voices know nothing of each other; an instrument plays voices together, and the engine plays the instrument.
- **A shared conductor.** Tempo, the grid of 16ths with its swing, bars, phrases, sections, the harmony's progression (including when a palette change may start), the audio tides and the playing of the instrument's score live in the engine rather than in an instrument, so several instruments can play in the same time, harmony and rhythm.
- **Typed signals.** Instead of a modulation matrix, a plain struct with one field per signal (`Signals`), written once per block: the conductor writes tempo, harmony and its changes, whether the bass is away, looseness and onsets; Strata's pulse writes the drone's dip, the chord gate and the bass accent; whoever needs a signal reads its field. Within an instrument, voices may still hand each other signals at audio rate (the drone follows the pulse sample by sample).
- **Presets.** An instrument's tuning constants are a plain data struct (`strata::Preset`, with settings for bed, pulse and space), so instruments in the web app can be presets of the same code; the sketches' polymeter variant already is one.
- **Fast in debug builds.** A package profile builds the crate with `opt-level` 2 in debug builds too. Instruments are an enum rather than trait objects, and the DSP stays plain code.

## Sketches

The sketches are an example of the audio crate, in `audio/sounddesign/sketches`; [Development](development.md#audio-output) has the commands. They render the instruments offline to `nova-audio-sketches` in the OS temporary directory, or to the directory in `NOVA_AUDIO_DIR`. Scripted scenarios set the controls as the sliders would, which the sound moves by the global tides as it does live, and hand over neutral gray light; the content modules are rendered by the real renderer through a bus, so they sound as the live instrument would.

Scenarios for Strata (`strata_…`): Heat, Form (in both rhythm variants), Void and Flow sweeps, every palette in turn, four corners (cold and warm, ambient and rhythmic), 10 minutes of harmonic motion, the light from the bottom to the top, and every content module with all controls at 0.5. Fathom has its own set (`fathom_…`): Heat with stabs, Form, Void and Flow sweeps, every palette, the four corners, 10 minutes of harmonic motion, 3 minutes of its throws for long listening, the light from the bottom to the top, from all voxels lit to none, from the left to the right, and every module at 0.5. For each it writes a 32-bit float stereo WAV at 48 kHz and a spectrogram (PPM, 20 Hz to 20 kHz on a logarithmic axis), and `report.md` lists the integrated loudness (ITU-R BS.1770) and loudness range, sample peak, crest factor, the share of energy per frequency band and the energy per octave band (the spectral balance), the side-to-mid ratio below 80 Hz (the mono check for the subwoofer), how much faster than real time the instrument rendered, the visual activity, and how many steps each pulse voice played.

The sketches measure about −21 LUFS for the drone and −19.5 LUFS with the pulse, peaks below −3 dBFS, and side signal more than 40 dB below the mid signal under 80 Hz. Fathom's drone measures about −20 LUFS like Strata's, its swells and stabs −17 to −21 LUFS depending on Form and Heat (Strata with the pulse −16.5 to −20), with peaks below −3 dBFS; at Void 0.5 its stabs rise about 12 dB above what remains between them (300 Hz to 3 kHz). On an Apple M2 they render about 50 times faster than real time in release builds, Fathom as fast as Strata. Debug builds optimize the audio crate too, which made the instrument 6 to 10 times faster there, close to its release speed. The reverb and the limiter are our own: an earlier FunDSP reverb, built from generic combinators, ran at 0.3 times real time in debug builds and made the stream drop out.

The measurements catch technical problems; they say nothing about whether it sounds good. Listening on the target system decides. Questions for listening:

- Does the drone have its own character, between cold and warm, without becoming a generic synth pad?
- Is the bass full but steady (no pumping from beating, no booming notes in the room)?
- Euclidean or polymeter: which rhythm feels intended rather than random?
- Do thumps, ticks and stabs sit in the drone, or on top of it? Is the dub delay too much or too little?
- Does Void at 0.5 sound like a neutral middle, and Void 1 far away but still present?
- Do the palettes give distinct tonal worlds, and do the crossfades (including the air resonators gliding) work?
- Are the modules distinguishable with all controls at 0.5, and is that coupling strong enough?
